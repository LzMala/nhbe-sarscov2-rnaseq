THE QUESTION

Airway cells meet viruses first. The cells in this project are primary human bronchial epithelial cells, shortened to NHBE. Primary means they came from a person's airway, not from a cancer cell line that has been growing in a lab for years.

The question is: 24 hours after SARS-CoV-2, which genes are higher or lower than in cells that got no virus, and did the cell's virus-alarm system actually turn on?

You did not infect these cells. Blanco-Melo and colleagues did, and they published the counts. Your project is a reanalysis of one small piece of that experiment.

The question is written into the top of the test script, scripts/03_de.py, as a note the computer skips:

  Tests which genes change in primary NHBE cells after SARS-CoV-2 versus mock.
  PyDESeq2, design ~ condition, mock as the reference. A positive log2 fold
  change means higher after infection.
  """

  The three quotes open and close a short description. It tells a reader the comparison before any number is calculated. "Mock as the reference" means no-virus is the baseline. "Positive log2 fold change" means the gene went up after infection. The test itself is written later, in Week 4.

WHAT THE CELLS DID

Each sample is a separate dish of cells.

  3 dishes    No virus. These are the mock samples.
  3 dishes    SARS-CoV-2, strain USA-WA1/2020, at a dose of 2 infectious particles per cell, collected 24 hours later.

Why three, not one? One dish cannot answer the question. If that dish looks different, the cause might be the virus, or it might be that one dish. A second dish helps, but one odd dish can still dominate. Three is the smallest number that lets you see whether the dishes agree. It is not a magic number. It is the number this experiment already had, and it is the usual minimum for this kind of study. More dishes would make the result firmer. Three is enough to do the test, and not enough to be very sure about small changes.

They measured messenger RNA, the working copies of genes. More messenger RNA usually means the cell is using that gene more. The measurement is a count: how many reads landed on that gene. The table has 21,797 genes and 6 dishes.

TWO GENE PROGRAMS

When a virus enters a cell, two conversations often start.

The inflammation conversation. A protein called NF-kB turns on genes that call other immune cells and raise inflammation. In your list those genes are IL6, TNF, CXCL8, and NFKBIA. NFKBIA is a brake on NF-kB. The cell turns that brake on when NF-kB has been active, so a rise in NFKBIA is a sign the pathway ran, not a sign it was off.

The interferon conversation. Type I interferon, especially IFN-beta (the gene IFNB1), is the cell's virus alarm. That alarm turns on antiviral genes, often called interferon-stimulated genes: IFIT1, IFIT2, IFIT3, ISG15, MX1, OAS1, RSAD2.

The published paper's claim for this kind of infection is: inflammation comes on strongly, and the interferon alarm is weak or uneven. Your Week 5 list was written to check that claim. The list has to be written before you look at the top genes. If you pick the genes after you see the winners, you can always build a flattering story.

The list is typed at the top of scripts/04_gene_panel.py, before that script reads any result.

WEEK 4, THE TEST

For each of the 12,291 genes, the test asks one question. After the dishes are put on the same scale, is this gene higher with the virus, or lower?

1. How big is the change?

Take one gene. Suppose its level is about 100 in the no-virus dishes and about 200 in the virus dishes. The ratio is 200 / 100 = 2. The gene is twice as high. That is a 2-fold rise.

The table stores the change as a log2 number, so a doubling and a halving are the same size with opposite signs.

  200 compared with 100 is twice as high. log2(2) = 1. The table writes +1.
  50 compared with 100 is half as high. log2(0.5) = -1. The table writes -1.

A plus means higher after infection. A minus means lower. The no-virus dishes are the baseline.

2. How much do the dishes wobble?

The three no-virus dishes never give the exact same count. The three virus dishes never do either. So for every gene the program writes two numbers.

  The change itself. This is the log2 fold change.
  How much that estimate wobbles from dish to dish. This is the standard error.

A gene with huge counts is steady, so its standard error is small. A rare gene bounces from dish to dish, so its standard error is wide. A wide standard error means the change is hard to trust.

The test then asks how big the change is next to that wobble:

  statistic = log2 fold change / standard error

A statistic near 0 means the change is small next to the wobble. A statistic past about 2 means the change is large next to the wobble.

3. Could the wobble alone have done this?

The p-value is that question turned into a chance. If the virus truly did nothing to this one gene, how often would dish-to-dish wobble still produce a statistic this large? A p-value of 0.05 means about 5 times in 100.

That bar is fair for one gene. You asked it 12,291 times. If the virus had changed none of them, about 5% of the tests would still fall under 0.05 by luck:

  12,291 x 0.05 = about 615 genes

Those 615 would look changed. They would be luck. A raw p-value of 0.05 is too loose once the question is asked for a whole genome.

Benjamini-Hochberg uses a stricter promise. Among the genes you declare changed, about 5 in 100 are expected to be luck. That rate is the false discovery rate. In the table this stricter number is the column padj, the adjusted p-value.

Here is the same rule on 5 genes, so the arithmetic is visible. In the real test the list has 12,291 genes. The steps match.

Sort the raw p-values from smallest to largest:

  0.001, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, 0.20

For the gene in position k, draw the line (k / 5) x 0.05:

  gene 1: 0.001 is under its line, 0.01
  gene 2: 0.01 is under its line, 0.02
  gene 3: 0.02 is under its line, 0.03
  gene 4: 0.04 sits on its line, 0.04
  gene 5: 0.20 is over its line, 0.05

Gene 4 is the last one still on its line. Genes 1 through 4 are kept. Gene 5 is left out.

The adjusted p-value is about: raw p-value x (number of genes / position in the sorted list). For gene 2 that is 0.01 x (5 / 2) = 0.025. The list is then tidied so an adjusted p-value never gets smaller as you move down. In the real table, a gene passes this part when its adjusted p-value is below 0.05.

4. Is the change large enough to care about?

A change can be steady and still tiny. A gene that goes from 1,000 to 1,100 is only about 10% higher. If all three dishes agree, the adjusted p-value can be very small. The second rule asks for a log2 fold change of at least 1, which is a doubling or a halving. That rule is a choice, made so the headline list is the larger changes. Both lists are saved, so the effect of the choice stays visible.

WEEK 6, ARE THE CHANGED GENES A KNOWN GROUP?

Week 5 looked at 12 genes you wrote down in advance. Week 6 looks at the whole winner list and asks a wider question. Do those winners belong to a process that already has a name?

Biologists keep lists of genes that share a job. One list is "TNF-alpha signaling through NF-kB." Another is "interferon-alpha response." Another is "IL-6 signaling." Week 6 checks your 95 genes that at least doubled against those ready-made lists.

1. The list test. Who is crowded inside the 95?

There are 12,291 tested genes. 95 of them at least doubled. Take one ready-made list, the TNF-alpha list. Count how many of its members sit inside those 95. Then ask whether that is more than you would expect from a list of that size.

A tiny example, so the counting is visible. These counts are an illustration. They are the shape of the test.

  The class has 100 genes.
  10 of them at least doubled. Those 10 are the winners.
  A ready-made inflammation list has 10 members in the class of 100.
  6 of those 10 members are inside the 10 winners.

Six out of ten winners belong to a list that is only ten out of a hundred genes in the class. The winners are crowded with inflammation genes. That crowding is the whole finding. The adjusted p-value asks how often a crowd like that would appear by luck, after many lists have been checked. A tiny adjusted p-value means the crowd is hard to explain as luck.

Your real list test, using the Hallmark collection:

  TNF-alpha signaling through NF-kB is the most crowded. Its adjusted p-value is about 10 to the minus 26. Write a decimal point, then 26 places, and the 1 is in the last place. That is a very small chance of luck.
  Inflammatory response and IL-6 signaling are crowded as well.
  Interferon-gamma response is crowded.
  Interferon-alpha response, the type I program from Week 5, is crowded too. Its adjusted p-value is about 10 to the minus 10. Still very small, and larger than the TNF-alpha value, so this group is behind TNF-alpha.

2. The ranked test. What about genes that rose, but rose less than double?

The list test ignores a gene that rose 1.5-fold. That gene failed the 2-fold rule in Week 4, and it can still be part of the interferon story. The ranked test lines up all 12,291 tested genes. The gene with the strongest rise is at the top. The gene with the strongest fall is at the bottom. It then asks whether the members of a ready-made list sit near the top together.

On that ordered list, TNF-alpha signaling is again at the front. Interferon-alpha is near the front with it. The genes that rose less than double still lean toward interferon. The list test remains the one that says which group is the most crowded among the genes that actually doubled.

Read together, the two tests say the same thing as the 12 names in Week 5. Inflammation, especially TNF-alpha through NF-kB, is the loudest named group. Interferon-alpha is present and sits behind it. The test matches your risen genes to lists that already have those names. It stops there. It cannot show that NF-kB was the cause.

THE SENTENCE FOR THE WHOLE PROJECT

In six dishes of primary bronchial cells, 95 genes rose and 16 fell at least 2-fold after SARS-CoV-2, with the false-discovery cutoff at 0.05. IL6 and TNF are clearly induced. IFN-beta itself is not measurably expressed. A few antiviral genes, especially MX1 and OAS1, do rise, and the interferon group is real but not the top group. That matches Blanco-Melo et al. 2020 in this cell type: inflammation is the louder program.

That sentence is carried by five files, each produced by the lines above.

      12,291 tested, 95 up, 16 down, 314 under the 2-fold line. From scripts/03_de.py.
